01 · The Position

BUILT FOR GROUPS, INVISIBLE TO THEM

This is not a property with a product problem. A $33 million renovation completed in 2022. 34,892 square feet of function space across 17 event rooms and 31 configurations. A Park Ballroom that seats 1,100 theater and 680 banquet. 490 guest rooms, 395 of them two-queen. Outdoor lawn and patio venues for 500–600. On-site bus parking. One block from the Anaheim Convention Center, walking distance to the Disneyland Resort.

Every one of those facts is a reason a planner should choose this hotel. The question is whether a planner ever encounters them at the moment they are deciding.

Group buyers do not shop the way transient guests shop. They search in plain language, by segment, months or years ahead, and they compare on published specifics.

Today, a sports coordinator searching for a tournament block near the Anaheim Convention Center, a couple searching for a ballroom that seats 680, and a tour operator searching for bus parking on Harbor Boulevard are all served the same brand page: one built for transient room sales, competing for attention with the rest of the Marriott estate, and syndicating capacity numbers that are not always current.

The program below fixes that in three moves: publish the specifics on a site built for group buyers, buy the exact searches those buyers make, and measure which segment produced which lead.

WHAT THE BUILD INCLUDES

  • One source of truth. A published property fact sheet covering capacities, configurations, renovation year, and parking, so that when a listing elsewhere disagrees, there is an authoritative page to correct it against.
  • Search engine optimization (SEO). Page-level meta strategy, on-page copy, internal linking architecture, and segment-level keyword targeting across all five pages.
  • Answer engine optimization (AEO). Schema.org Hotel, EventVenue, and FAQ structured data embedded on every page, so ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude, and Google's AI surfaces return your capacity figures rather than outdated syndicated ones.
  • Measurement-first lead capture. The RFP form routes directly to the group sales office with full source attribution (GA4 events plus lead tracking), so every submission arrives tagged with the segment page and campaign that produced it.
  • Maintenance for the full term. Content updates, capacity corrections, page additions, and conversion-rate work throughout the engagement.
Brand compliance: The concept uses property photography from the Marriott.com listing and AI-animated motion clips for demonstration only. The production build replaces these with videos approved by the property team, and the final site is subject to Marriott franchise digital guidelines.

SEGMENT SEQUENCING · ONE SEGMENT AT A TIME

Doubled Rook's recommendation is to run 100% weddings at launch, add meetings and conventions once the group base is restored, and open the remaining segments after that. All five pages go live at launch and earn organic traffic from day one. Paid budget goes to one segment at a time.

WHY CONCENTRATE RATHER THAN SPREAD

Split five ways, a $1,500 monthly budget puts roughly $300 behind each segment. At wedding and venue click costs that is fewer than fifty clicks per campaign per month, which is below the volume any bidding algorithm needs to leave its learning phase. Five underfunded campaigns do not average out to one funded one. They all stall.

Concentrating the full budget on a single segment clears that threshold, produces enough conversion data to optimize against within the first term, and gives your leadership team one clean read on whether the channel works before it is asked to fund a wider program.
